# Brand

The code of Hey Postie is free software. The brand is a trademark matter. This page draws the line.

## What the brand is

- the name **Hey Postie**, and any name that could be confused with it;
- the logo;
- Postie, the postman character (cap, satchel, bike), in all his moods, and the illustrations that feature him (`packages/ui/src/illustrations/`);
- the domain `hey-postie.com` and the project's social accounts.

Snouzylabs S.R.L., the publisher of Hey Postie, claims the name and Postie as its marks. A trademark filing is planned; until it is granted, the name is an unregistered mark.

## How this fits with the AGPL

The source files of the logo and the illustrations are part of the program and ship under the AGPL like the rest, so that anyone can build and run the app. That license gives you copyright permissions. It gives you no right to use these signs to say that something is Hey Postie, or comes from Hey Postie, when it does not: [LICENSE-EXCEPTION.md](LICENSE-EXCEPTION.md) declines trademark rights, as section 7(e) of the AGPL allows.

## What you may do

- Say that your project uses or is based on Hey Postie's code, and link here.
- Keep the name where the license requires it: copyright notices, license texts, git history.
- Show screenshots of Hey Postie in articles, reviews, talks and courses.
- Package the unmodified software for a distribution or a package manager under the name Hey Postie, as long as it stays unmodified and points back here.

## What you may not do without written permission

- Distribute a fork, a modified build or a hosted copy under the name Hey Postie or a name that could be confused with it.
- Use the logo, Postie or the illustrations to identify your own product, site or service, or in its marketing.
- Present your version as official, or as endorsed by, affiliated with or supported by Hey Postie.
- Register domains, handles or app-store listings that use the name.

## If you fork

Pick your own name, replace the logo and Postie wherever a user sees them, and remove the name from the user-facing text (`packages/ui/src/i18n/`, the product name and identifier in `apps/desktop/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json`). Leave the license notices untouched. The AGPL asks that your users can get your source; the brand asks that they can tell your project from this one.
